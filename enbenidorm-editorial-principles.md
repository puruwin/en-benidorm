# enBenidorm.es — Editorial Principles

## 1. Purpose

enBenidorm.es should become a useful local resource, not a mass-generated SEO site.

Content Lab exists to help produce content that is:

- locally relevant,
- evidence-backed,
- specific,
- useful for real decisions,
- auditable,
- maintainable.

Search visibility is a goal, but it must follow usefulness.

---

## 2. Do not confuse data availability with recommendation quality

A business with:

- a good website,
- structured data,
- a large menu,
- many extractable facts

is not automatically a better recommendation.

Likewise, a local business with:

- no official website,
- weak structured data,
- poor online documentation

is not automatically a bad recommendation.

Editorial selection must answer:

> Is this business genuinely useful or interesting for the reader's intent?

not:

> Is this business easy for our crawler to document?

---

## 3. Discovery is not recommendation

The system should preserve distinct states.

Conceptually:

```text
Discovered
    ↓
Topic candidate
    ↓
Topic confirmed
    ↓
Editorially eligible
    ↓
Cohort passes gate
    ↓
Generation
    ↓
QA
    ↓
Publication
```

A business can be real and topically relevant yet still not deserve inclusion.

No candidate should disappear silently. Exclusion must retain a reason.

---

## 4. Evidence hierarchy

Prefer:

1. official local source,
2. official brand source explicitly tied to the location,
3. open structured data,
4. reviewed manual observation,
5. third-party supporting evidence.

Never silently promote lower-confidence evidence into higher-confidence evidence.

---

## 5. Local scope matters

Brand-level evidence does not prove that a local establishment:

- offers the same menu,
- has the same hours,
- is open,
- supports the same services,
- has the same prices.

Location-specific claims require location-specific evidence or explicit reviewed attribution.

---

## 6. Operational status

Do not state that a business is open unless current evidence supports it.

Do not state that a business is closed based only on weak or conflicting third-party evidence.

Use:

- confirmed,
- unknown,
- needs verification,
- conflict

when appropriate.

---

## 7. Comparisons need meaningful differentiation

A comparison is not useful merely because each business has three populated data dimensions.

Dimensions should help a user choose.

Useful signals may include:

- genuinely different offer,
- specific signature items,
- price positioning,
- location/use case,
- schedule,
- dietary options,
- atmosphere/format,
- booking/access constraints,
- other topic-specific differences.

Generic fields should not simulate editorial depth.

---

## 8. Topic relevance versus general context

Evidence should distinguish:

### Topic anchor

Direct proof that the business is relevant to the query/topic.

### Choice signal

Information that helps compare it against other businesses.

### General context

Useful background that does not itself make the comparison stronger.

Example for breakfast:

- “serves breakfast” = topic anchor
- “Full English breakfast €4.90” = strong choice signal
- “located in Levante” = context / sometimes choice signal
- “has takeaway” = usually generic context unless relevant to the topic

---

## 9. Gates are safeguards, not targets

Do not optimize the data to make the gate pass.

Do not:

- invent facts,
- stretch dimensions,
- duplicate one fact across dimensions,
- reduce thresholds solely to publish,
- include weak businesses just to reach a minimum count.

If a comparison cannot meet the standard, it should remain blocked.

---

## 10. Generation rules

Generation must use only approved evidence.

It should avoid:

- generic praise,
- unsupported superlatives,
- “best” claims without basis,
- repeating business descriptions,
- implying first-hand experience when none exists,
- presenting observed prices as permanently current.

When prices are used, preserve observation context/date where appropriate.

---

## 11. Manual observations

Manual evidence is legitimate when clearly labelled and dated.

It should be used for things that were actually observed.

It should not be used as a convenient way to fill missing fields.

---

## 12. SEO strategy

The project should prefer:

- fewer good pages,
- strong internal linking,
- meaningful local coverage,
- useful comparison pages,
- high-quality business profiles,
- clear provenance and maintainability

over bulk generation.

Manual indexing should focus on pages that meet the quality bar.
